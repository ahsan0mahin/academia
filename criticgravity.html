1️⃣ What is New Criticism? — সহজ ভাষায়

📗 FROM THE BOOK (Tyson's Chapter)

New Criticism is a literary theory that says: "The text itself is the only thing that matters."

When you read a poem, story, or novel, you should focus only on the words on the page. You should NOT care about:

  • The author's life or what the author intended to say
  • The historical period when it was written
  • Your personal feelings or emotional reactions
  • The political or social context

According to Tyson, New Criticism dominated American literary criticism from the late 1930s through the 1970s. It was the first theory many students learned and its method of close reading is still widely used today, even by critics who don't call themselves New Critics.

🇧🇩 বাংলায় বুঝি

New Criticism বলে: "শুধু text-এ কী লেখা আছে সেটাই দেখো। লেখক কে, কখন লিখেছে, তুমি পড়ে কী feel করছো — এসব matter করে না।"

ধরো তুমি একটা কবিতা পড়ছো। New Critic বলবে — কবির জীবনী পড়ো না, কবিতার যুগ নিয়ে ভাবো না। শুধু কবিতার ভেতরে কী আছে — words, images, metaphors, structure — এগুলো analyze করো।

এটা অনেকটা এরকম — তুমি একটা painting দেখছো। New Criticism বলবে: "শুধু painting-টা দেখো। painter-এর biography পড়ো না।"

🎯 EXAM TIP

Exam-এ যদি জিজ্ঞেস করে "What is New Criticism?" — তাহলে প্রথমেই বলবে: "New Criticism is a formalist approach to literature that focuses exclusively on the text itself, examining its formal elements such as imagery, symbolism, paradox, irony, and the interrelationship of these elements to create organic unity." তারপর explain করবে।

The Core Idea in One Line:

"A literary text is an autonomous work of art — a self-contained, self-referential object."

— Central belief of New Criticism, as presented in Tyson

🇧🇩 বাংলায়

একটি সাহিত্যকর্ম (literary text) নিজেই সম্পূর্ণ — এটা বোঝার জন্য বাইরের কোনো কিছুর দরকার নেই। Text নিজেই নিজের meaning তৈরি করে।

2️⃣ Important Concepts/Terms — Identified & Explained

Tyson's chapter-এ যে গুরুত্বপূর্ণ concepts আছে, সেগুলো এখানে আলাদা করে চিহ্নিত করা হলো:

📗 Book

1. Close Reading

The careful, detailed analysis of a text's formal elements — every word, image, and literary device. This is the primary method of New Criticism.

🇧🇩 Text-কে খুব কাছ থেকে, একটা একটা শব্দ ধরে ধরে পড়া এবং analyze করা। প্রতিটি word, image, symbol-এর meaning বের করা।

📗 Book

2. Organic Unity

The idea that every element in a literary text (plot, characters, imagery, symbols, etc.) works together to create a single, unified meaning. Nothing is extra; everything is connected.

🇧🇩 একটা ভালো সাহিত্যকর্মে সবকিছু একসাথে কাজ করে — কোনো element আলাদা না। যেমন শরীরের প্রতিটি organ একসাথে কাজ করে, তেমনি text-এর প্রতিটি part একসাথে একটি unified meaning তৈরি করে।

📗 Book

3. Intentional Fallacy

The error of judging a text based on what the author intended to say. Tyson explains that for New Critics, the author's intention is irrelevant — only the text matters. The meaning is in the text, not in the author's mind.

🇧🇩 "লেখক কী বোঝাতে চেয়েছিলেন" — এটা দিয়ে text বিচার করা ভুল। কারণ text-ই সব। লেখকের মাথায় কী ছিল সেটা আমরা জানি না, আর জানার দরকারও নেই।

📗 Book

4. Affective Fallacy

The error of judging a text based on the reader's emotional response. Just because a poem makes you cry doesn't mean it's good literature. The value of a text is in its formal qualities, not in how it makes readers feel.

🇧🇩 "পড়ে আমার কেমন লাগলো" — এটা দিয়ে text বিচার করা ভুল। তুমি কাঁদলে বা হাসলে সেটা text-এর literary value নির্ধারণ করে না।

📗 Book

5. The Text as Autonomous Object

The text exists on its own. It is independent from the author, the reader, and the historical context. Think of a text like a "well-wrought urn" — a beautiful object that exists by itself.

🇧🇩 Text স্বয়ংসম্পূর্ণ — বাইরের কোনো কিছু ছাড়াই এটি নিজে নিজে complete। এটিকে একটি স্বতন্ত্র শিল্পকর্ম হিসেবে দেখতে হবে।

📗 Book

6. Literary Language vs. Scientific Language

Tyson explains that New Critics believed literary language is fundamentally different from scientific or everyday language. Scientific language is direct and has one meaning. Literary language is rich, complex, and has multiple meanings through ambiguity, paradox, irony, and metaphor.

🇧🇩 বিজ্ঞানের ভাষা সরাসরি — "পানি ১০০° তে ফুটে।" কিন্তু সাহিত্যের ভাষা জটিল — একটি শব্দে অনেক meaning থাকে। "My love is a rose" — এখানে rose মানে শুধু ফুল না, সৌন্দর্য, ক্ষণস্থায়িত্ব, কাঁটা — অনেক কিছু।

📗 Book

7. Ambiguity

When a word, phrase, or passage has more than one meaning, and all those meanings contribute to the richness of the text. For New Critics, ambiguity is not a weakness — it's a sign of literary quality.

🇧🇩 একটি শব্দ বা বাক্যের একাধিক অর্থ থাকা। New Critics-এর কাছে এটা দুর্বলতা না, বরং শক্তি — কারণ এতে text আরও rich হয়।

📗 Book

8. Paradox

A statement that seems contradictory but reveals a deeper truth. For New Critics, paradox is central to how literary language works — it holds opposing ideas together.

🇧🇩 যেটা দেখতে স্ববিরোধী (contradictory) মনে হয়, কিন্তু আসলে গভীর সত্য প্রকাশ করে। যেমন: "The child is father of the man" — বাচ্চা কিভাবে বাবা হয়? কিন্তু এর গভীর অর্থ আছে।

📗 Book

9. Irony

When there is a contrast between what is said and what is actually meant, or between expectation and reality. New Critics valued irony because it adds complexity and depth to a text.

🇧🇩 যখন বলা হচ্ছে একটা, কিন্তু আসল অর্থ অন্য কিছু। বা যখন আশা করা হচ্ছে একটা, কিন্তু ঘটছে তার উল্টো। New Critics irony-কে ভালোবাসতো কারণ এটা text-কে complex করে তোলে।

📗 Book

10. Tension

The stretching between opposing meanings, ideas, or elements within a text. Tension is created by the interplay of conflicting elements — and for New Critics, the best literature holds these tensions together without resolving them too easily.

🇧🇩 Text-এর ভেতরে বিপরীত ideas-এর মধ্যে টানাটানি। যেমন love vs. duty, appearance vs. reality — এই conflict text-কে interesting করে তোলে। ভালো সাহিত্য এই tension-কে ধরে রাখে।

📗 Book

11. The Heresy of Paraphrase

Tyson discusses this concept (from Cleanth Brooks): you cannot simply paraphrase a poem's meaning into a plain statement. The meaning of a literary text is inseparable from its form — HOW it says something is part of WHAT it says. If you paraphrase a poem, you lose the poem.

🇧🇩 তুমি একটা কবিতার মানে সহজ ভাষায় বলতে পারো না — কারণ কবিতার meaning তার form-এর সাথে জড়িত। তুমি যদি "Shall I compare thee to a summer's day?" কে বলো "তুমি কি সুন্দর?" — তাহলে কবিতাটাই হারিয়ে গেলো। Form আর content আলাদা করা যায় না।

3️⃣ Deep Dive — প্রতিটি Concept বিস্তারিত

📖 Close Reading — Full Breakdown

DEFINITION:

Close reading is the careful, sustained analysis of a literary text that focuses on the text's formal elements — such as images, symbols, metaphors, rhyme, meter, point of view, setting, characterization, plot — and their contribution to the text's overall meaning (its organic unity).

মূল IDEA:

Instead of reading a text quickly for "what happens," you read it slowly, word by word, paying attention to HOW it creates meaning. You ask: "Why did the author use THIS word instead of another? What does this image suggest? How does this metaphor connect to the theme?"

কেন IMPORTANT:

Close reading is the fundamental method of New Criticism. Without close reading, you cannot do New Critical analysis. Tyson emphasizes that even after New Criticism lost its dominance, close reading remained the foundational skill for literary study.

কীভাবে কাজ করে:

Step 1: Read the text carefully, multiple times. Step 2: Identify literary devices (metaphor, simile, irony, paradox, symbols, imagery). Step 3: See how these devices relate to each other. Step 4: Show how they all contribute to a single, unified theme (organic unity).

EXAMPLE (from Tyson's analysis of The Great Gatsby):

📗 Tyson demonstrates a New Critical reading of The Great Gatsby by analyzing how the novel's imagery, symbolism, and narrative structure all work together to produce the theme of the American Dream's corruption. She examines the green light, the eyes of Doctor T.J. Eckleburg, the valley of ashes, and other formal elements to show how they create organic unity around this central theme.

🎯 EXAM-এ কীভাবে ব্যবহার করবে:

যখন exam-এ কোনো passage দিয়ে বলবে "Analyze this passage" — তখন close reading-এর technique ব্যবহার করো। প্রতিটি literary device identify করো এবং দেখাও কীভাবে সব মিলে একটা unified meaning তৈরি করে।

🔗 Organic Unity — Full Breakdown

DEFINITION:

Organic unity means that all the formal elements of a literary text — plot, characterization, imagery, symbolism, tone, point of view, setting, theme — work together harmoniously, like parts of a living organism. Each part is necessary and contributes to the whole.

মূল IDEA:

A great literary work is not just a collection of parts — it's an organic whole where everything fits together. Remove one element and the whole meaning changes. The form and content are inseparable.

কেন IMPORTANT:

This is the ultimate goal of New Critical analysis — to demonstrate that a text has organic unity. If you can show that all elements work together to produce a unified meaning, you've successfully done a New Critical reading.

কীভাবে কাজ করে:

When analyzing a text, you identify various formal elements (imagery, symbols, plot, etc.) and then show how they ALL point toward the same central theme or meaning. The connections between these elements create the organic unity.

EXAMPLE:

📗 In Tyson's New Critical reading of The Great Gatsby, she shows how the green light (Gatsby's hope/dream), the valley of ashes (dream's corruption), the eyes of Eckleburg (moral emptiness), and Nick's narrative perspective all work together organically to produce the unified theme: the American Dream is fundamentally corrupt.

🎯 EXAM-এ কীভাবে ব্যবহার করবে:

Answer-এর conclusion-এ always বলো: "Thus, through the interplay of [elements you discussed], the text achieves organic unity around the theme of [central theme]." এটা New Critical answer-এর best conclusion।

🚫 Intentional Fallacy — Full Breakdown

DEFINITION:

The intentional fallacy is the mistaken belief that the meaning of a literary text can be determined by looking at the author's stated or assumed intention. According to New Critics, the text's meaning is in the text itself, not in what the author meant to say.

মূল IDEA:

Once a text is written, it becomes an independent object. The author's plan, diary entries, letters, interviews — none of these should determine how we interpret the text. The "words on the page" speak for themselves.

কেন IMPORTANT:

This concept liberates the text from the author. It says that critics should analyze what the text DOES, not what the author WANTED it to do. This made literary criticism more objective and text-focused.

🇧🇩 ধরো Shakespeare একটা চিঠিতে লিখলেন "Hamlet-এ আমি X বোঝাতে চেয়েছি।" New Critic বলবে — "সেটা irrelevant। Hamlet-এ আসলে কী আছে সেটাই matter করে। Shakespeare যা বোঝাতে চেয়েছিলেন আর Hamlet যা actually বলছে — দুটো আলাদা জিনিস হতে পারে।"

🎯 EXAM-এ কীভাবে ব্যবহার করবে:

Short question-এ directly define করো এবং বলো কেন New Critics এটাকে fallacy বলেন। Broad question-এ এটাকে New Criticism-এর key rejection হিসেবে explain করো।

💔 Affective Fallacy — Full Breakdown

DEFINITION:

The affective fallacy is the error of evaluating a literary text based on the emotional effects it produces in the reader. New Critics argue that the reader's feelings are subjective and unreliable — they cannot serve as the basis for objective literary analysis.

মূল IDEA:

"I cried when I read this poem, so it must be great" — this is the affective fallacy. Your tears don't make it great literature. What makes it great (or not) is its formal qualities — its use of language, imagery, structure, etc.

কেন IMPORTANT:

This keeps literary criticism objective. Different readers feel different things — so emotional response is too subjective to be a valid critical tool. By rejecting the affective fallacy, New Critics tried to make literary criticism more like a science.

🇧🇩 ধরো একটা গান শুনে তোমার খুব কষ্ট হলো। কিন্তু সেটা কি প্রমাণ করে যে গানটা musically great? না। তোমার ব্যক্তিগত আবেগ text-এর literary quality নির্ধারণ করে না। New Critics বলতো — আবেগ সরাও, text-এর ভাষা আর structure দেখো।

🎯 EXAM TIP:

Intentional Fallacy আর Affective Fallacy-কে একসাথে compare করো exam-এ — দুটোই বলছে text-এর বাইরের জিনিস (author's intent / reader's emotion) দিয়ে text বিচার করা ভুল।

⛪ The Heresy of Paraphrase — Full Breakdown

DEFINITION:

Coined by Cleanth Brooks, the "heresy of paraphrase" means that the meaning of a literary text CANNOT be reduced to a simple paraphrase or summary. The way something is said (form) is inseparable from what is said (content).

মূল IDEA:

If you could simply paraphrase a poem's meaning into a plain sentence, then why would the poem exist? The poem exists because its meaning can ONLY be expressed in that particular form. Form = Content.

EXAMPLE:

Take Keats' line: "A thing of beauty is a joy forever." You could paraphrase it as "Beautiful things make people happy for a long time." But the paraphrase loses the rhythm, the music, the weight of the original words. The MEANING of the line includes its form.

🇧🇩 রবীন্দ্রনাথের "আমার সোনার বাংলা, আমি তোমায় ভালোবাসি" — এটার মানে কি শুধু "I love Bangladesh"? না! ওই specific শব্দ, ওই সুর, ওই ছন্দ — সব মিলে meaning তৈরি হয়। Paraphrase করলে মূল জিনিসটাই হারিয়ে যায়।

🔬 Literary Language vs. Scientific Language — Full Breakdown

DEFINITION:

New Critics made a sharp distinction between literary (poetic) language and scientific (referential) language. Scientific language has one clear, fixed meaning. Literary language is rich, complex, ambiguous, and has multiple layers of meaning.

কীভাবে কাজ করে:

Scientific Language Literary Language
One fixed meaningMultiple meanings (ambiguity)
Direct, clearIndirect, complex
Can be paraphrasedCannot be paraphrased (heresy of paraphrase)
InformationalExperiential
"Water boils at 100°C""My love is like a red, red rose"

4️⃣ New Critics এবং তাদের Ideas/Terms

📗 FROM THE BOOK — Tyson-এর chapter-এ যেভাবে এসেছে

🧑‍🏫 John Crowe Ransom

Key Contribution: Coined the term "New Criticism" with his 1941 book The New Criticism. He helped establish the movement and gave it its name.

Key Idea: Advocated for treating literature as an object of study in itself, separate from historical and biographical approaches.

🇧🇩 "New Criticism" নামটাই দিয়েছিলেন তিনি। তিনি বলেছিলেন সাহিত্যকে নিজের merit-এ বিচার করতে হবে, ইতিহাস বা জীবনী দিয়ে নয়।

🧑‍🏫 Cleanth Brooks

Key Contributions:

  • The Heresy of Paraphrase — You cannot reduce a poem's meaning to a simple paraphrase
  • The Well Wrought Urn (1947) — His famous book of New Critical analyses
  • Emphasized paradox and irony as key elements of poetic language
  • Co-authored Understanding Poetry (with Robert Penn Warren), a textbook that taught close reading to generations of students

🇧🇩 Brooks-ই বলেছিলেন কবিতাকে paraphrase করা "heresy" (ধর্মদ্রোহিতা/পাপ)। তিনি paradox আর irony-কে কবিতার প্রাণ মনে করতেন। তাঁর Understanding Poetry বইটি close reading শেখানোর bible ছিল।

🧑‍🏫 W.K. Wimsatt & Monroe Beardsley

Key Contributions:

  • The Intentional Fallacy (1946) — Argued that the author's intention should not determine a text's meaning
  • The Affective Fallacy (1949) — Argued that the reader's emotional response should not determine a text's value

These two essays are foundational to New Criticism because they remove both the author and the reader from the equation, leaving only the text.

🇧🇩 এই দুজন মিলে লেখক আর পাঠক দুজনকেই সরিয়ে দিলেন। Intentional Fallacy-তে বললেন — লেখকের উদ্দেশ্য matter করে না। Affective Fallacy-তে বললেন — পাঠকের আবেগ matter করে না। শুধু text-ই থাকলো।

🧑‍🏫 Robert Penn Warren

Key Contribution: Co-authored Understanding Poetry and Understanding Fiction with Cleanth Brooks. These textbooks made close reading the standard method of teaching literature in American universities.

🇧🇩 Brooks-এর সাথে মিলে Understanding Poetry বই লিখেছিলেন যেটা American university-তে close reading শেখানোর standard textbook হয়ে গিয়েছিল।

🧑‍🏫 Allen Tate

Key Contribution: Developed the concept of tension in poetry — the interplay between the literal and figurative meanings of words.

🇧🇩 কবিতায় শব্দের literal (আক্ষরিক) আর figurative (রূপক) অর্থের মধ্যে যে tension থাকে — সেই concept তিনি develop করেছিলেন।

📙 ADDITIONAL INFO (বইয়ের বাইরে)

I.A. Richards — Often considered a precursor to New Criticism. His work on practical criticism and close analysis of texts influenced the New Critics, though he was not strictly a New Critic himself. Tyson mentions the broader intellectual context but the degree of coverage of Richards varies by edition.

Irony Gap between what is said and what is meant, or between expectation and reality বলা আর বোঝানো-র মধ্যে পার্থক্য Adds complexity; New Critics highly valued irony in texts
Tension The pull between opposing elements/meanings within a text বিপরীত উপাদানের মধ্যে টানাটানি Creates depth; associated with Allen Tate
Intentional Fallacy Error of using author's intention to determine meaning লেখকের উদ্দেশ্য দিয়ে অর্থ নির্ধারণের ভুল Removes author from interpretation
Affective Fallacy Error of using reader's emotions to judge a text's value পাঠকের আবেগ দিয়ে মূল্যায়নের ভুল Removes reader from evaluation
Heresy of Paraphrase A poem's meaning cannot be reduced to a simple paraphrase; form = content কবিতার অর্থ সরল ভাষায় বলা সম্ভব না; রূপ আর বিষয়বস্তু অবিচ্ছেদ্য Central concept by Cleanth Brooks; supports text autonomy

6️⃣ Assumptions, Characteristics, Principles & Method

📌 Core Assumptions (বইয়ের ভিত্তিতে)

  1. The literary text is autonomous — It exists independently from author, reader, and historical context.
  2. The text is the most important thing — Only the "words on the page" matter for interpretation.
  3. Literary language is special — It is different from and richer than everyday or scientific language because of its ambiguity, paradox, irony, and complexity.
  4. Form and content are inseparable — HOW something is said is part of WHAT it means. You cannot separate them.
  5. Great literature has organic unity — All elements work together to create a unified whole.
  6. The meaning of a text is timeless and universal — Good literature speaks to all people across all time periods. The meaning doesn't change based on when you read it.

🇧🇩 সহজ কথায়: New Criticism ধরে নেয় যে — text নিজেই সব, text-এর ভাষা বিশেষ, form আর content এক, ভালো সাহিত্যে সবকিছু একসাথে কাজ করে, এবং সাহিত্যের অর্থ চিরকালের জন্য।

📌 Key Characteristics

🔍 Text-Centered

All analysis focuses on the text itself

🔬 Objective

Tries to be scientific and impersonal

📝 Formalist

Focuses on form, structure, literary devices

🏛️ Anti-Historical

Ignores historical/social context

🚫 Anti-Biographical

Ignores the author's life

♾️ Universalist

Believes great literature is timeless

📌 Key Principles

  1. The Primacy of the Text: The text is a self-sufficient object. External factors are irrelevant.
  2. Close Reading is the Method: Careful analysis of formal elements is the only valid approach.
  3. Organic Unity is the Goal: A successful analysis shows how all elements work together.
  4. Form = Content: What a text says cannot be separated from how it says it.
  5. Author's Intention is Irrelevant: (Intentional Fallacy)
  6. Reader's Emotion is Irrelevant: (Affective Fallacy)
  7. Literary Language is Unique: It operates through ambiguity, paradox, irony, and tension.

📌 Method of Analysis (কীভাবে New Critical Analysis করবে)

📗 Tyson describes the New Critical method through her sample analysis of The Great Gatsby:

1

Read the text carefully (Close Reading)

Read multiple times. Focus on the words on the page.

2

Identify Formal Elements

Find imagery, symbols, metaphors, irony, paradox, tone, point of view, narrative structure, etc.

3

Analyze the Interrelationships

Show how these elements relate to each other. How does the imagery connect to the symbolism? How does irony create tension?

4

Demonstrate Organic Unity

Show that ALL these elements work together to produce a single, unified theme/meaning.

5

Avoid External References

Do NOT bring in author biography, historical context, or personal feelings.

🇧🇩 সহজ কথায়: Text বারবার পড়ো → Literary devices খুঁজে বের করো → দেখাও কীভাবে সব devices একসাথে কাজ করে → প্রমাণ করো সব মিলে একটা unified meaning তৈরি করে → বাইরের কিছু আনবে না।

7️⃣ What New Criticism Rejects & Why

❌ Rejects: Author's Biography/Intention

Why? Because the author's life and intention are EXTERNAL to the text. The text must speak for itself. Using the author's diary or letters to interpret a text is the Intentional Fallacy.

🇧🇩 লেখকের জীবনকাহিনী বা উদ্দেশ্য text-এর বাইরের জিনিস। Text নিজেই কথা বলুক — লেখকের মুখের দিকে তাকানোর দরকার নেই।

❌ Rejects: Reader's Emotional Response

Why? Because emotions are subjective — different readers feel differently. If we judge a text by emotion, we have no objective standard. This is the Affective Fallacy.

🇧🇩 আবেগ ব্যক্তিগত — একজন কাঁদে, আরেকজন হাসে। আবেগ দিয়ে বিচার করলে কোনো objective standard থাকে না।

❌ Rejects: Historical/Social Context

Why? Because the text is autonomous. Its meaning is timeless and universal. Tying it to a specific historical period limits its universal significance.

🇧🇩 Text চিরকালের — একে কোনো নির্দিষ্ট যুগে বেঁধে রাখলে এর universal significance কমে যায়।

❌ Rejects: Political/Ideological Reading

Why? Because using a text to promote political or social ideas turns literature into propaganda. New Critics wanted to keep literature "pure" — focused on aesthetic/formal qualities.

🇧🇩 সাহিত্যকে রাজনৈতিক বা সামাজিক প্রচার (propaganda) হিসেবে ব্যবহার করলে সাহিত্যের নিজস্ব শিল্পমূল্য নষ্ট হয়। New Critics সাহিত্যকে "শুদ্ধ" রাখতে চেয়েছিলেন।

❌ Rejects: Paraphrase as Interpretation

Why? Because the meaning of a literary text is embedded in its form. You cannot extract the meaning and put it in plain words without losing the essence. This is the Heresy of Paraphrase.

🇧🇩 সাহিত্যের অর্থ তার রূপের (form) মধ্যে আটকানো। সরল ভাষায় বললে আসল জিনিসটা হারিয়ে যায়।

🎯 EXAM TIP

যদি প্রশ্ন আসে "What does New Criticism reject?" — তাহলে এই 5টি point মনে রাখো: Author's intention, Reader's emotion, Historical context, Political reading, Paraphrase. মনে রাখার shortcut: A-R-H-P-P (Author, Reader, History, Politics, Paraphrase).

📗 FROM THE BOOK

Tyson's book is structured so that each chapter presents a different critical theory. In the New Criticism chapter, she implicitly and sometimes explicitly contrasts New Criticism with other approaches:

🇧🇩 মূল পার্থক্য: New Criticism শুধু text দেখে। অন্যান্য theory text-এর বাইরে দেখে — কেউ লেখক দেখে, কেউ পাঠক দেখে, কেউ সমাজ দেখে, কেউ ইতিহাস দেখে। New Criticism-এর বিশেষত্ব হলো এটা বলে: "বাইরের কিছু দরকার নেই।"

📙 ADDITIONAL CONTEXT

Interestingly, Deconstruction (which came after New Criticism) also does close reading — but reaches the opposite conclusion. Where New Criticism says close reading reveals unified meaning, Deconstruction says close reading reveals that meaning is always unstable and contradictory. Tyson discusses this in her Deconstruction chapter.

9️⃣ Difficult Passages — Paraphrased & Explained

Difficult Idea #1: "The Text as Autonomous Object"

"For New Critics, a literary text is an autotelic artifact — that is, a self-contained, self-referential aesthetic object that exists for its own sake, independent of the conditions that produced it, the psychological responses it produces in readers, or the political, moral, or philosophical programs it might be said to support."

PARAPHRASE (Easy English):

New Critics believed that a literary text is a complete, independent object — like a vase or a painting. It exists on its own. You don't need to know who made it, when it was made, how it makes you feel, or what political message it carries. You just look at the object itself and study its qualities.

🇧🇩 New Critics মনে করতো একটা literary text হলো একটা self-contained object — যেমন একটা মূর্তি। মূর্তিটা কে বানিয়েছে, কখন বানিয়েছে, তুমি দেখে কী feel করো — এসব দরকার নেই। শুধু মূর্তিটার নিজের সৌন্দর্য দেখো। "Autotelic" মানে "নিজের মধ্যেই নিজের উদ্দেশ্য আছে" — text নিজেই নিজের জন্য exist করে।

Difficult Idea #2: Form and Content Inseparability

"For New Criticism, the form and content of a literary work are inseparable: what a text says and how it says it are viewed as one thing, not two. To try to separate them by, for example, summarizing a poem's 'theme' in a sentence or two, is to commit what Cleanth Brooks calls 'the heresy of paraphrase.'"

PARAPHRASE (Easy English):

For New Critics, the content (what a text says) and the form (how it says it) cannot be separated. They are ONE thing. If you try to summarize a poem's theme in simple words, you destroy the poem — because the poem's meaning IS its form. Brooks called this mistake the "heresy of paraphrase" — it's like a sin against literature.

🇧🇩 ধরো একটা কবিতায় লেখা আছে: "Shall I compare thee to a summer's day?" এর theme কী? "তুমি সুন্দর"? কিন্তু এই summary তো কবিতার আসল রস-ই দেয় না! কারণ কবিতার meaning শুধু theme-এ না — ওই নির্দিষ্ট শব্দ, ছন্দ, রূপকল্প — সবকিছু মিলে meaning তৈরি হয়। Form আর content আলাদা করা যায় না — এটা Brooks-এর মূল কথা।

Difficult Idea #3: Timeless, Universal Meaning

"New Critics believe that the timeless meaning of a text — what the text says about the human condition — is accessible to any 'competent' reader who gives the text sufficient time and attention through close reading."

PARAPHRASE (Easy English):

New Critics believed that great literature has a meaning that never changes — it is true for all people, at all times, in all places. And any reader who is skilled enough and reads carefully enough can find this meaning. The meaning is "in" the text, waiting to be discovered through close reading.

🇧🇩 New Critics বিশ্বাস করতো যে ভালো সাহিত্যের meaning চিরকালের — ১০০ বছর আগেও same, ১০০ বছর পরেও same। Shakespeare-এর Hamlet আজও relevant কারণ এর meaning universal — সব মানুষের জন্য প্রযোজ্য। যেকোনো "যোগ্য" পাঠক close reading করে এই meaning বুঝতে পারবে।

⚠️ সমালোচনা: এই ধারণাটি পরবর্তী তাত্ত্বিকরা (Marxists, Feminists, Postcolonialists) challenge করেছেন — তাঁরা বলেন "universal" meaning আসলে white, male, Western perspective থেকে দেখা meaning।

1️⃣4️⃣ Model Answer #1

QUESTION: Discuss the basic assumptions and principles of New Criticism.

INTRODUCTION

New Criticism is a formalist approach to literature that emerged in the United States in the 1930s and dominated literary criticism until the 1970s. As Lois Tyson explains in Critical Theory Today, the central belief of New Criticism is that the literary text is an autonomous, self-sufficient object whose meaning can be discovered through close reading of its formal elements. Unlike other approaches that consider the author's biography, historical context, or the reader's emotional response, New Criticism focuses exclusively on "the words on the page."

BODY — Paragraph 1: Text Autonomy

The most fundamental assumption of New Criticism is that the literary text is an autonomous object. According to the New Critics, a text exists independently from the author who wrote it, the historical period in which it was produced, and the readers who interpret it. The text is self-contained and self-referential — its meaning is embedded within its own formal structure. This assumption leads to two key principles: the Intentional Fallacy and the Affective Fallacy, proposed by W.K. Wimsatt and Monroe Beardsley. The Intentional Fallacy warns against using the author's stated or assumed intention to determine a text's meaning, while the Affective Fallacy warns against judging a text based on the emotional response it produces in readers.

BODY — Paragraph 2: Close Reading & Formal Elements

The primary method of New Criticism is close reading — the careful, detailed analysis of a text's formal elements such as imagery, symbolism, metaphor, irony, paradox, ambiguity, and tension. New Critics believe that literary language is fundamentally different from scientific or everyday language. While scientific language has one fixed, clear meaning, literary language is rich, complex, and multi-layered. The literary devices of ambiguity, paradox, irony, and tension are what make literary language special and give it the ability to express complex human experiences that cannot be conveyed through plain prose.

BODY — Paragraph 3: Organic Unity & Form-Content

Another crucial principle is organic unity — the idea that all formal elements of a literary text work together harmoniously to create a single, unified meaning, much like the organs of a living body work together to sustain life. Closely related to this is the principle that form and content are inseparable. Cleanth Brooks expressed this through his concept of the "heresy of paraphrase," arguing that a poem's meaning cannot be reduced to a simple paraphrase because the form (how something is said) is an integral part of the content (what is said). To paraphrase a poem is to destroy it.

CONCLUSION

In conclusion, New Criticism rests on several interconnected assumptions: the autonomy of the text, the irrelevance of authorial intention and reader response, the special nature of literary language, the inseparability of form and content, and the ideal of organic unity. While later critical approaches have challenged many of these assumptions — particularly the claim of timeless, universal meaning — New Criticism's legacy endures in the practice of close reading, which remains a foundational skill in literary analysis to this day.

1️⃣4️⃣ Model Answer #2

QUESTION: Explain the Intentional Fallacy and the Affective Fallacy with their significance.

INTRODUCTION

Two of the most influential concepts in New Criticism are the Intentional Fallacy and the Affective Fallacy, both proposed by W.K. Wimsatt and Monroe Beardsley. These two concepts are foundational because they effectively remove both the author and the reader from the process of literary interpretation, leaving only the text as the object of analysis.

BODY — Intentional Fallacy

The Intentional Fallacy, proposed in 1946, argues that it is an error to judge a literary text based on the author's stated or assumed intention. According to Wimsatt and Beardsley, once a text is written, it becomes an independent object. The author's diary entries, letters, interviews, or personal statements about what they "meant" to say are irrelevant to the text's meaning. The meaning is in the text itself — in its words, images, and structure — not in the author's mind. For example, even if a poet says "I intended this poem to be about love," the text itself might reveal themes of loss, mortality, or something entirely different. The text speaks for itself, regardless of the author's intention.

BODY — Affective Fallacy

The Affective Fallacy, proposed in 1949, argues that it is equally wrong to evaluate a literary text based on the emotional effects it produces in the reader. Just because a poem moves a reader to tears does not make it great literature; just because a novel bores a reader does not make it a failure. The reader's emotional response is subjective and varies from person to person, making it an unreliable basis for literary evaluation. The quality of a text should be judged by its formal properties — its use of language, imagery, structure, and how these elements create organic unity — not by how it makes individual readers feel.

BODY — Significance

Together, these two fallacies are significant because they establish the text as the sole focus of literary analysis. By removing the author (Intentional Fallacy) and the reader (Affective Fallacy), New Critics created a framework in which literary criticism could be more objective and systematic. The critic's job is not to guess what the author meant or to describe how the text makes them feel, but to analyze the text's formal properties through close reading. This approach made literary criticism more rigorous and gave it a methodology that could be taught, learned, and applied consistently.

CONCLUSION

In conclusion, the Intentional Fallacy and the Affective Fallacy are twin pillars of New Critical theory. By arguing against the use of authorial intention and reader response in literary interpretation, Wimsatt and Beardsley helped establish the text as an autonomous, self-sufficient object of study. While later approaches such as Reader-Response criticism have challenged the Affective Fallacy by placing the reader at the center of interpretation, these concepts remain important contributions to the history of literary theory.
